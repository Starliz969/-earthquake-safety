Earthquake Safety Tips For People With Disabilities

1. Keep Assistive Equipment Within Reach

Keep wheelchairs, walking sticks, hearing aids, glasses and other assistive devices close to where residents usually sit or sleep. Make sure these items are easy to find during an emergency. Caregivers should also know where each person's equipment is kept.

2. Prepare Individual Emergency Plans

Every resident may have different needs, so one emergency plan cannot work for everyone. Caregivers should prepare individual plans that explain how each person communicates, moves and receives assistance. Important medical information should also be easy to access.

3. Use Different Emergency Warning Methods

Do not depend only on loud alarms. Some residents may not hear them, while others may have difficulty understanding them. Use visual warning signs, written messages, vibrating alerts and clear verbal instructions to make sure everyone receives the warning.

4. Keep Evacuation Routes Clear

Remove furniture, boxes and other objects that could block hallways or doorways. Make sure evacuation routes are wide enough for wheelchairs and walking aids. Caregivers should also identify accessible exits and safe meeting points before an earthquake happens.

5. Provide Safe Assistance During the Shaking

Residents who can move independently should protect their head and neck and take cover under sturdy furniture when possible. Wheelchair users should lock their wheels if safe to do so and protect their head and neck. Caregivers should avoid pulling or lifting residents unless it is necessary and safe.

6. Prepare Medicine and Essential Supplies

Keep necessary medicines, medical documents, water, food, hygiene supplies and basic first-aid items in an accessible emergency kit. Check supplies regularly and make sure that important medical equipment has a backup power plan when needed.

7. Communicate and Provide Emotional Support

During an earthquake, some residents may feel confused, frightened or overwhelmed. Caregivers should speak calmly, use simple instructions and allow residents time to respond. Written messages, pictures or familiar communication methods can also help people understand what is happening.

8. Check on Everyone After the Earthquake

After the shaking stops, caregivers should check whether residents are safe and need medical attention. Be prepared for aftershocks, avoid damaged buildings and follow official instructions. Do not return to unsafe areas until authorities say it is safe.

Safety Guide for Different Disabilities

Every person experiences emergencies differently. Click on each category to discover how caregivers can provide suitable support during an earthquake.

Visual Disabilities

Possible Challenges

  • Difficulty identifying fallen objects and other hazards.
  • Difficulty locating emergency exits and unfamiliar routes.
  • Difficulty reading written emergency instructions.

How Caregivers Can Help

  • Give clear verbal directions and describe nearby obstacles.
  • Keep walking sticks and other mobility aids within reach.
  • Ask before offering assistance and allow the person to hold your arm if they prefer.
  • Keep pathways clear of furniture and fallen objects.
Hearing Disabilities

Possible Challenges

  • Not hearing emergency alarms or verbal warnings.
  • Difficulty receiving instructions when people are speaking from a distance.
  • Missing important announcements after the earthquake.

How Caregivers Can Help

  • Use flashing lights, visual alarms or vibrating alerts.
  • Write down important instructions or use simple signs.
  • Get the person's attention before communicating.
  • Keep hearing aids and spare batteries accessible when possible.
Physical or Mobility Disabilities

Possible Challenges

  • Difficulty moving quickly during an emergency.
  • Blocked pathways may prevent wheelchair users from moving safely.
  • Stairs may be difficult or impossible to use.

How Caregivers Can Help

  • Keep wheelchairs, walking sticks and other mobility aids nearby.
  • Make sure hallways and exits are accessible and uncluttered.
  • Prepare an individual emergency plan with suitable assistance.
  • Never attempt to carry or lift someone without knowing the safe method.
Cognitive or Learning Disabilities

Possible Challenges

  • Difficulty understanding complicated emergency instructions.
  • Feeling overwhelmed by loud noises or sudden changes.
  • Needing extra time to process information and respond.

How Caregivers Can Help

  • Use short, simple instructions and explain one step at a time.
  • Use pictures, symbols or familiar signs to communicate.
  • Stay calm and give the person enough time to understand.
  • Practise emergency routines beforehand so the instructions become familiar.
